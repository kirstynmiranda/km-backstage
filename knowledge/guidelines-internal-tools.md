# Vinted voice and tone - internal tools

This tone guide explains how to modulate Vinted's general tone of voice when writing content for Vinted's internal tools. It is written for content designers working on internal-facing products. If any guidance here conflicts with Vinted voice and tone - general guidelines, defer to this document.

---

## Context

Internal tools are used exclusively by Vinted staff to carry out operational, moderation, support, and content management tasks. Unlike consumer or B2B products, internal tools have no brand impression function — users are colleagues, not members or external business partners. The priority is always efficiency, clarity, and accuracy.

This guide covers content written for the following internal tools:

* **Vinted moderation platform:** Used by Vinted's moderation team to review marketplace content for safety and policy compliance.
* **Vinted Admin:** Vinted's CMS backend, used to manage and deploy content including member inbox notices and help centre articles.
* **Vinted Ticketron:** Vinted's internal support ticketing system, used to manage incoming member support tickets and related workflows.

> **Note on terminology:** Internal tool users are Vinted employees, not members. Use "you/your" for second-person address. Refer to the people Vinted serves as "members" (consistent with the wider Vinted brand standard), not "users", "customers", or "end users."

---

## Tone modulation

Internal tool content uses Vinted's three voice pillars, but in a significantly flatter, more functional register than any consumer-facing product. The goal is not to express brand personality — it is to help colleagues do their jobs accurately and efficiently.

1. **Practical (Foundation):** The dominant pillar across all internal tools, all of the time. Every string must serve a functional purpose: orienting the user, communicating status, or driving a specific action. Remove anything that does not do one of these things.
2. **Familiar (Minimal accent):** Internal tools should feel human enough not to be cold or robotic, but Familiar should never come at the expense of precision or speed. Think of it as the tone of a clear, direct colleague — not a customer-facing experience.
3. **Joyful (Not used):** Joyful is not appropriate for internal tools. Staff using these tools are working, not being delighted. Celebratory or energising language creates noise in operational contexts and should be avoided entirely.

> **Key distinction from consumer and B2B products:** Even in consumer success states, Vinted uses Joyful to reward and motivate members. Internal tools have no equivalent moment. A moderation decision actioned, a ticket resolved, or an article published are routine work outcomes — not milestones to celebrate.

### Additional tone principles

**Internal tool content should always:**
* Be scannable. Internal tool users process high volumes of content quickly. Use short labels, clear headings, and consistent patterns so colleagues can orient themselves at a glance.
* Be unambiguous. Vague status labels or action descriptions create operational errors. Every label, button, and status should have exactly one interpretation.
* Use consistent language across tools where workflows overlap. For example, if a moderation action in the moderation platform triggers a ticket in Ticketron, the language describing that action should be consistent across both surfaces.
* Respect the user's expertise. Internal users are trained professionals. Do not over-explain standard workflows or add instructional copy that experienced users do not need.

**Internal tool content should never:**
* Use consumer-facing or brand-forward language (e.g. "Great work!", "You're all set!", "Let's go!").
* Be unnecessarily terse or cold. Clarity does not require unfriendliness — a direct, collegial tone is always appropriate.
* Use ambiguous status language. Statuses like "pending", "in progress", "resolved", and "escalated" must mean the same thing everywhere they appear.
* Pad labels or button text with unnecessary words. In high-volume operational tools, every extra word is friction.

**Writing voice — active vs. passive:**

Use the active voice as the default for actions, instructions, and labels. Use the passive voice only in these situations:

* To describe system-generated outcomes (e.g. "Ticket automatically assigned.")
* To describe actions taken by another team or tool (e.g. "Flagged by automated review.")
* To communicate status neutrally without implying who acted (e.g. "Case closed.")

## Tool-by-tool tone guidance

While the overall register is consistent across all three tools, the nature of the work in each tool warrants specific tone considerations.

### Vinted moderation platform

Moderators review marketplace content and make decisions that directly affect members — including content removal, account restrictions, and escalations. Copy in this tool must support fast, confident decision-making without introducing ambiguity or emotional weight.

**Specific guidance:**
* **Action labels must be unambiguous and outcome-oriented.** A moderator should be able to read a button label and know exactly what will happen when they click it. Avoid generic labels like "Submit" or "Confirm" — prefer "Remove listing", "Restrict account", "Escalate to senior moderator."
* **Do not use language that implies judgement of the member.** Moderation is a policy and safety function, not a moral one. Use neutral, outcome-focused language (e.g. "Listing removed: policy violation" not "Listing removed: seller broke the rules").
* **Status labels must be precise.** Moderators work across high volumes of cases. Statuses like "Under review", "Actioned", "Escalated", and "Closed" must be used consistently and defined clearly in any onboarding or help documentation.
* **Destructive or irreversible actions require clear signposting.** If an action cannot be undone (e.g. a permanent account restriction), copy must make this explicit before the action is confirmed. Use plain language: "This action cannot be undone."

### Vinted Admin

Vinted Admin is used to create, manage, and publish content that members will see — including inbox notices and help centre articles. Content designers using this tool are often the direct authors of member-facing copy, so Admin's interface copy must support careful, considered content work.

**Specific guidance:**
* **Support the content review process.** Admin interface copy (labels, statuses, prompts) should make it easy to understand the state of a piece of content at a glance — drafted, in review, scheduled, published, archived.
* **Be precise about publishing actions.** Publishing or updating member-facing content is consequential. Action labels and confirmation prompts should make clear what is being published, to whom, and when. Avoid vague labels like "Push live" — prefer "Publish to all members" or "Schedule for [date]."
* **Distinguish clearly between draft and live content.** Any interface element that could cause a content designer to confuse a draft with a published item is a content risk. Status labels, banners, and visual indicators should make the content state immediately obvious.
* **Errors should be specific.** If a publish action fails or a required field is missing, the error message should identify exactly what went wrong and how to fix it — not just that something went wrong.

### Vinted Ticketron

Ticketron is used to manage incoming member support tickets. Support agents work through high volumes of tickets daily, so copy must support fast triage, clear status tracking, and efficient handoffs between agents or teams.

**Specific guidance:**
* **Triage and status labels must be instantly readable.** Support agents should be able to scan a queue and understand ticket status, priority, and ownership without reading full descriptions. Labels like "Open", "Pending member reply", "Escalated", "Resolved" must be used consistently.
* **Action labels should describe the outcome, not the mechanics.** Prefer "Assign to billing team" over "Reassign", and "Request more information" over "Send message."
* **Handoff copy must be complete.** When a ticket is transferred between agents or teams, any copy accompanying the handoff (automated notes, status updates) should give the receiving agent everything they need to continue without going back to the original agent.
* **Avoid language that could be copied into member-facing replies by mistake.** Internal notes, system labels, and operational copy should be clearly distinguished from any copy that might be sent to members. Where possible, internal-only elements should be visually or structurally distinct — and copy should never include internal shorthand that would be confusing or inappropriate if seen by a member.

## Tone mapping by situation

| Context | Primary pillar | Secondary pillar | Joyful allowed? | Other characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action labels and buttons** | Practical | Practical | ❌ No | Outcome-oriented. Describe exactly what happens. Never generic. |
| **Status labels** | Practical | Practical | ❌ No | Consistent across tools. Unambiguous. One interpretation only. |
| **Error messages** | Practical | Familiar | ❌ No | Specific about what went wrong. Clear about how to fix it. Never blame. |
| **Destructive action confirmations** | Practical | Practical | ❌ No | State the action, the consequence, and whether it is reversible. Plain language only. |
| **Empty states** | Practical | Familiar | ❌ No | Explain what the space is for. Brief. No filler. |
| **Onboarding and first use** | Practical | Familiar | ❌ No | Orient the user quickly. Respect their expertise. No hand-holding. |
| **System notifications** | Practical | Practical | ❌ No | State what happened and what (if anything) the user needs to do. |
| **Success states** | Practical | Practical | ❌ No | Confirm the outcome factually. No celebration. |

### Tone "Volume" logic

* **Action labels and buttons:** Volume 0% Familiar, 0% Joyful.
* **Status labels:** Volume 0% Familiar, 0% Joyful.
* **Error messages:** Volume 15% Familiar, 0% Joyful.
* **Destructive action confirmations:** Volume 0% Familiar, 0% Joyful.
* **Empty states:** Volume 15% Familiar, 0% Joyful.
* **Onboarding and first use:** Volume 20% Familiar, 0% Joyful.
* **System notifications:** Volume 0% Familiar, 0% Joyful.
* **Success states:** Volume 0% Familiar, 0% Joyful.

## Cross-tool consistency

Several workflows touch more than one internal tool — for example, a moderation action in the moderation platform may generate a ticket in Ticketron, or a member inbox notice drafted in Vinted Admin may be triggered by a moderation outcome. Where workflows cross tool boundaries, copy must be consistent.

**Guidelines for cross-tool content:**
* **Use the same terminology for shared concepts.** If a status, action, or entity (e.g. a "case", a "ticket", a "notice") appears in more than one tool, it should be named the same way in both.
* **Do not assume context carries over.** Each tool is used by different teams who may not have visibility into what happened in another tool. Copy that references a cross-tool action should include enough context to be understood without switching tools.
* **Flag cross-tool terminology conflicts during design.** If the same concept is named differently across tools, raise it as a content issue — do not work around it with additional explanatory copy.

# Vinted product writing style guide

This guide is the single source of truth for how we stylise content when building products at Vinted. It’s simple enough for anyone to use, whether you’re a designer, engineer, or a product manager.

## Core writing principles 

Our writing principles help us apply the three pillars of our voice to the copy we write for the Vinted product. 

* **Member-first** \- At Vinted, members always come first. That’s why we write content that makes them feel like they’re our main focus. Because when members feel like they’re our priority, they trust us more. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Don’t worry, this transaction will be reviewed.  Add your phone number for extra security.  There was an issue, but your details are safe.  |  |

* **Write delight into the everyday \-** Be on the lookout for opportunities where you can delight without letting it get in the way of the main task. Ultimately, it’s OK to make an experience feel human and lighthearted when the context allows it. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Find new faves\!  Your wardrobe refresh  Keep what you love. List the rest before March for a fresh start.  | Browse more items to buy   Clean out your closet |

* **Keep it concise \-** We keep it short and sweet because we respect our members’ time. Our goal is to get them where they need to go as quickly as possible. We reduce cognitive load by cutting out unnecessary words. Keep experiences feeling straightforward, but don’t let “short” become boring or robotic**.** 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Item listed List another/ List another item  | You’ve successfully listed an item   |

* **Stay inclusive \-** We carefully choose words that make everyone feel welcome. This means avoiding gendered language, ableist terms, and cultural idioms that might alienate parts of our global community. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| *Find new faves\!*  | *Keep the momentum going*  |

## Writing foundations

Foundations are the building blocks of content that help us make sure our copy stays clear, accessible, and practical across all screens. 

Follow these rules to keep Vinted: 

* **Consistent** \- Use the same words (or approved synonyms) and patterns to reduce cognitive load  
* **Helpful** **\-** Use content to help members achieve their goals   
* **Cohesive** **\-** Create a cohesive experience for members throughout the product 

### Active voice 

We almost always use an active voice at Vinted. With an active voice, it’s always clear who’s doing what. It also keeps our copy direct, easier to scan, and conversational. 

* **Be transparent \-** Use an active voice in order to communicate with members clearly 

* **Be straightforward \-** Use simple and straightforward content to keep Vinted experiences transparent and practical 

| *✅ We would say* | *❌ We wouldn’t say*  |
| :---- | :---- |
| *Verify your identity  List an item  Cancelled transaction* | *Identity verification  New item listing  Transaction was cancelled*  |

* **Take responsibility \-** We recognise when something is our fault and share appropriate details so members understand the situation. Using an active voice helps us demonstrate to members that we own up to mistakes and inconveniences, instead of distancing ourselves through passive, robotic language. 

* **Build trust \-** We turn cold processes into warm interactions that feel human, which makes members feel like they can trust Vinted 

| *✅ We would say* | *❌ We wouldn’t say*  |
| :---- | :---- |
|  *Don’t worry, our team will review this transaction.*  |  *This transaction will be reviewed.*  |

### Passive voice 

In certain contexts, it makes more sense to use passive voice. Use passive voice whenever the **status of an item** is more important than the **member acting on it.** 

* **Focus on the item \-** Use passive voice when an item is the “main character” of a message  
    
* **Status updates don’t need to be active \-** Sometimes, passive voice helps keep things short and simple. For example, having “Item listed” as a header is far more practical than “We have listed your item.”  
    
* **Design for constraints \-** Use passive voice in small spaces. Passive voice works well when providing a quick summary or update in a small space (For example, in a toast or a snackbar). 

| *✅ We would say* | *❌ We wouldn’t say*  |
| :---- | :---- |
|  *Your favourited item **\[item name\]** was sold Item listed*  |  ***\[member name\]** bought your favourited **\[item name\]** We have listed your item*  |

### Pronouns

We use different pronouns depending on the context. In general: 

* Use **“you”** to encourage action  
* Use **“we”** to show support  
* Use **“my”** to clarify whether something is general or specific to a member (My listings/Listings)   
* Use **“they/them”** when referring to another member in the transaction 

#### **Addressing the member (You)** 

* **Second person \-** Use second person when addressing members to keep it conversational (you, your) 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Your** parcel is on its way to the buyer\! | Parcel on its way to the buyer |

#### **Referring to Vinted as a company (We)** 

Use “we,” “our,” and “us” when writing as the company. This helps members feel like there are human beings behind the brand**.**

* Use “we” when the system is performing a task for a member, like verifying their personal information, transferring money to a member, or contacting support on their behalf

* **Stay in the background \-** Avoid using “Vinted” as the subject, unless it’s related to privacy, compliance, or part of a specific product name (For example, “Vinted Balance”)

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Our** platform  Please wait until **our** support team contacts you.  You don’t have to do anything. **We’ll** update you.  | Vinted platform  You will be contacted by the support team soon.  No action required at this time.  |

#### **Personal account details and consent (My)** 

There are certain areas in a member’s account that are personal, like the badges they’ve earned or items they’ve listed for sale. In these cases, we use first person to give members that feeling of ownership.

 Additionally, using first person also makes it clear to a member that we’re referring to their personal area. For example, we use “my listings” to help members distinguish between items they’ve listed and general listings on Vinted. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| **My** Orders  **My** Listings  | Your Orders Your Listings / Listings |

* **Legal \-** When asking for consent, use first person for clarity, as this is a legal requirement (For example, “Yes, I agree”) 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
|  I certify that I am 18 years or older I confirm that I have read Vinted’s Privacy Policy  I agree to Vinted’s Terms and Conditions  |  You certify that you are 18 years or older  Agree to Vinted’s Terms and Conditions  |
 

#### **Talking about other members (They/\[member name\])** 

* **Call them “members” \-** We refer to people who buy or sell on the Vinted platform as “members.” If there is a transaction taking place, you can call them “buyers” and “sellers.” Avoid using other terms like “user” or “customer.” 

* **Be gender-neutral \-** Use “they/them” and in general avoid pronouns to keep the platform inclusive 

* **Use third person \-** Use third-person perspective when talking about another member who is part of a transaction   
    
* **Use member names \-** Sometimes, it’s OK to mention a member’s username to add extra clarity and a personal touch


| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| **\[member name\]** added your “\[product name\]” to **their** favourites  How was your experience with this **member**?  | **\[member name\]** added your “\[product name\]” to **her** favourites.  How was your experience with this **user**?  |

### Use everyday language

Vinted content is written in UK English first and then localised into other languages. Stick to plain, clear language that anyone can understand, regardless of whether they’re native English speakers and what their literacy level is.  

The best way to test UI copy is the “face-to-face” test. If you wouldn’t say a sentence to a friend, don’t use it on Vinted. 

* **Read it out loud \-** Watch out for any phrases that sound robotic. If it sounds formal or awkward, it needs to be simplified.   
    
* **Use [contractions](#contractions) \-** Using contractions helps make what you write sound like everyday speech   
    
* **Swap formal for normal \-** Avoid language that makes Vinted sound cold or robotic. Always choose the simplest words to get the message across.   
    
* **Write for a global audience \-** Avoid idioms, jargon, and slang that non-native English speakers may not understand. They’re not easy to localise into other languages. Also, Vinted represents an international audience, and not everyone has English as their first language, so keep it simple and literal. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Leave feedback for the “\[item name\]” you bought from \[member name\].  | Keep the momentum going Provide feedback for your latest purchase.  |

* **Be consistent \-** For the most part, avoid using synonyms. To learn which terms and synonyms are OK to use in each product area, check the [Writer's guild glossary].   
    
* **Lead with the most important part \-** Put the most important information first so members get the message immediately while scanning   
    
* **Get to the point \-** If a word doesn’t help a member understand what to do next, get rid of it 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Get your prepaid shipping label.  | Generate a prepaid shipping label for your parcel.  |

* **Don’t use ‘helper’ words that don’t add meaning \-** In most cases, cut out ‘helper’ words like “sorry” or “please” that don’t add meaning to what you’re trying to communicate

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Your favourited item was sold | Sorry, but the item you favourited was sold to another member. |

There are two specific situations where using “Sorry” and “Please” are OK: 

* **Bad outcomes \-** We say “sorry” to communicate to Vinted members that a bad outcome is our fault. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| We’re sorry that this delivery didn’t go as you expected. | We’re sorry the seller cancelled your order. |

* **Extra effort required \-** We say “please” when asking someone at Vinted to put in extra effort to complete a task or solve an error

## UX writing patterns 

### Progressive disclosure 

Progressive disclosure means giving members the exact information they need, when they need it. Instead of overwhelming members with all the content at once, details are revealed gradually. 

* **Prioritise the essentials** \- Show only the information required to complete the current task   
    
* **Use descriptive titles and buttons \-** Make it easy for members to scan for the information they need 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
|  |  |

* **Hide extra information \-** Keep help text and edge cases outside of the main interface to avoid distractions. Instead, use tooltips or “Learn more” links, so members can easily find additional information if they need it. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
|  |  |

### Be device-agnostic {#be-device-agnostic}

As Vinted members move between phones, tablets, and computers, this experience should feel seamless. This means the same content needs to work everywhere, regardless of the size or layout of a screen. 

* **Describe the intent, not the gesture \-** Use universal verbs like “Select” or “Choose” instead of hardware-specific terms like  “Tap” or “Click” 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Translate this conversation  Translate this  View  | Tap to translate  Click to translate  Tap to view  |

* **Avoid directional language \-** Don’t refer to the location of UI elements or components, since layouts can change on different screen sizes   
    
* **Reference the UI element, not its location \-** Refer to the “Menu,” “Settings,” or “Search bar” by name, not by where they are on the screen


| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Go to Inbox  Check Vinted Balance | Find your Inbox on the bottom menu Check your Vinted Balance above |

### In-line links

Use links to provide secondary information without cluttering the main message on the screen. 

* **Place the link on the subject \-** The link text should clearly describe what happens when a member clicks on it. Don’t link the entire sentence.   
    
* **Don’t link the punctuation \-** If the linked text is part of a full sentence, don’t hyperlink the punctuation 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Take the labelled parcel to the nearest dropoff point.  | Take the labelled parcel to the nearest dropoff point. |

*In this example, the link navigates to a list of nearby dropoff points based on a member’s location.* 

We use descriptive links to tell members exactly what’s going to happen next. Descriptive links make the product easier to navigate and keep Vinted accessible for members using screen readers and other assistive technologies. 

* **Be specific \-**  When possible, choose specific, action-oriented words instead of vague labels like “Read more” or “Learn more.” 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Take the labelled parcel  to the nearest dropoff point. | Take the labelled parcel  to the nearest dropoff point. Learn more |


* **Manage space constraints \-** In cases where space is an issue, general labels are OK. Just make sure the person seeing the label has enough context. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| List another item  Translate this conversation  | List Translate  |


* **Underlining links \-** Underlining is exclusively reserved for hyperlinks so members know where to click 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Take the labelled parcel to the nearest drop-off point | Take the labelled parcel to the nearest **drop off point**  |

* **Avoid multiple links** \- Avoid using multiple links in a single screen, since it can create cognitive overload for members 

### Conciseness and hierarchy 

* **Avoid repetitive copy \-** Don’t say the same exact thing in the header, body text, and button. However, a button can and should complement the main action mentioned in the title. 

* **Make it scannable** \- Focus on writing what is useful in order to highlight important information. If a member quickly scans just the title and the button of a dialog, they should have enough information about what will happen once they select that button. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Title:** Still interested?  **Body paragraph:** Your accepted offer is ready when you are. **Button:** View  | **Title:** Still interested in this offer?  **Body paragraph:** Your offer has been accepted. Are you still interested in buying this item?  **Button:** View offer   |

* **Lead with the most important information \-** Put the most important information at the start of the sentence so members can quickly find what they are looking for 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Title:** Is your issue resolved?  **Body paragraph:** We’ll release the payment to the seller and the order will be completed. You can’t undo this and won’t be able to raise another issue.  **Button:** Mark as resolved  | **Title:** Has your issue been resolved?  **Body paragraph:** Has your issue been resolved? If so, the payment will be released to the seller and the order will be marked as completed. This cannot be undone.  **Button:** Yes, it’s been resolved |

#### **Tips for staying concise** 

| Cut out unnecessary filler words  |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| Very Actually Really Basically Simply | For the most part Slightly Sort of  Only  |

| Use short words and phrases  |  |
| :---- | ----- |
| Your item was successfully listed → Item listed This transaction has been suspended → Transaction suspended   |  |

| Use questions  |
| :---- |
| Let us know if you didn’t receive a code → Didn’t get a code?  Indicate whether you’re still interested in the item you favourited → Still interested?  |

## Grammar and mechanics 

### British spelling 

Use UK spelling for all member-facing content. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Pick up the parcel from a neighbour | Pick up the package from a neighbor  |

* When using an abbreviated country or region as an adjective, don’t include punctuation 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| The US  EU  | The U.S.  E.U.  |

### Capitalisation 

We use sentence-case for *almost* everything. It makes copy easier to read and helps keep our tone familiar and conversational. 

#### **Sentence case** 

* Use for headings, buttons, labels, menu items, category names, and placeholder text 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Other men’s clothing Search for items Got it  | Other Men’s Clothing  Search for Items  Got It  |

#### **Title case**

* Use for proper nouns, Vinted product names, and badge names 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| View my Listings  Choose your Bump  Stand out with Showcase Speedy Shipping badge  | View My listings  Choose your bump  Stand out with showcase  Speedy shipping badge  |

#### **Lowercase** 

* Use all lowercase when referring to seasons (spring, summer) and currencies (euros, pounds) 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Pay in euros  Get ready for spring  | Pay in Euros  Get ready for Spring  |


### Contractions 

Use contractions occasionally. Contractions make Vinted feel less robotic and more conversational. 

* **Use common contractions \- “**You’re,” “didn’t,” “wasn’t,” and “it’s” are all examples of well understood contractions and OK to use. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| Tell buyers what you’re selling  | Tell buyers what you are selling  |

* **Avoid contractions on nouns** \- It can make messages less clear and harder to localise   
    
* **Avoid contractions that are hard to read \-** Contractions like “you’ve” or “they’ve” can make texts harder to read. If you stumble while saying it outloud, try finding a simpler way to get your message across. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| *Your parcel is on its way to the buyer.*  | *Your parcel’s on its way to the buyer.*  |

| Approved contractions |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| Aren’t  Can’t  Couldn’t  Didn’t  Doesn’t  Don’t  Hasn’t  Haven’t  Isn’t  It’s  Let’s  Shouldn't That’s  | There’s They’re They’ve  Wasn’t  We’ll We’re  Weren’t  What’s  Where’s  Won’t  You’ll  You’re You’ve  |

### Contractions to avoid

| Contractions to avoid |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| You’ve  They’ve  Mustn’t  |  |


### User confirmations (“OK” and “Got it”)

* Use all caps when writing “OK” in buttons   
* When we simply want a Vinted member to acknowledge something, use “Got it” as button text to keep it conversational. 

| *✅ We would say* |  *❌We wouldn’t say* |
| :---- | :---- |
| OK, close  Got it | Ok, close  OK |

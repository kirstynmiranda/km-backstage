# Product Email Content Guidelines

These guidelines apply to all product-related emails (transactional, lifecycle, nudges, announcements).

# 1\. Subject Line & Preheader

## 1.1 Subject line

**Goal:** Clearly set expectations and motivate an open without feeling “salesy” or click‑baity. Typical scenarios when a system email is sent:  
a) the member needs to do a task.  
b) the member is getting important information they need to read.  
c) the member is receiving an update they need to know about.

**Tone & style**

* Be clear and concrete: say what the email is about.  
* Front‑load the key information (what, then why).  
* Use sentence case (e.g., “Your weekly report is ready”).  
* Avoid spammy language (“Free\!\!\!”, “Act now\!\!\!”, all caps).

**Personalization**

* Use the customer’s first name only when it adds clarity or warmth (“Alex, your invoice is ready”), not in every send.  
* Do not include sensitive information (account numbers, security codes, etc.).

**Character limits**

* Aim for 35–50 characters.  
* Hard limit for localisation: max 60 characters (longer subjects get truncated on mobile).

**Emojis**

* Default: no emojis in subject lines.  
* Allowed only for clearly brand‑aligned, non‑critical messages (e.g., light feature announcements), and at most one emoji at the end or beginning.  
* Never use emojis for security, billing, or urgent/system emails.

## 1.2 Preheader (Preview text)

**Goal:** Complement the subject with one clear supporting detail, not a repeat.

**Tone & style**

* Expand or clarify the subject (“See what changed in your workspace this week”).  
* Avoid boilerplate (“View in browser”, “You’re receiving this email because…”).

**Character limits**

* Aim for 40–80 characters.  
* Put the most important words first; many phones truncate after \~80 characters.

**Content rules**

* Do not repeat the subject line verbatim.  
* Can include one short benefit, next step, or key detail.  
* Avoid emojis unless mirroring an approved emoji in the subject.

**Examples**  
Subject: You’ve got a new message  
Preheader: Open the chat to reply and keep your sale moving.

Subject: Someone’s interested in your item  
Preheader: Check your messages and answer their questions.

# 2\. Email Title (In‑Email Heading)

**Goal:** Confirm for the reader that they’re in the right place and what this email is about.

**Tone & style**

* Use a clear, descriptive H1 heading at the top of the email.  
* Use sentence case (not Title Case) unless brand standards say otherwise.  
* Make it action‑oriented or outcome‑oriented where possible.

**Character limits**

* Aim for 25–50 characters.  
* Hard limit for localisation: max 70 characters to avoid wrap and clutter on mobile.

**Content rules**

* The title should align with but not copy the subject line.  
* Focus on what this email helps the user do (e.g., “Review your invoice for August”).  
* Avoid internal jargon or feature code names.

**Examples**

* Your item has been sold  
* Confirm your email address  
* A favourite item is available again

# 3\. Greetings (“Hi {First name}”)

**Goal:** Use greetings when they add clarity or warmth, not by default.

**Use a greeting when:**

* The email has a narrative or explanatory body (product updates, onboarding, tips, educational content).  
* The email feels like a message from a person or team (“Hi Alex,”).

**Skip the greeting when:**

* The email is purely transactional or system‑like (e.g., OTP codes, password resets, security alerts, receipts).  
* The body is very short and action‑only (“Here’s your verification code”).

**Style**

* Default format when greeting is used: “Hi {username}”  
  


# 4\. Buttons (Primary Actions)

**Goal:** Make the primary action unmistakable, and keep each email focused on one main thing. For more on buttons r[ead full guidelines here](https://zeroheight.com/2b590eb39/v/latest/p/32a4e9-button/b/838e31). 

**When to use a button**

* There is one primary action we want the user to take (e.g., “Reset password”, “View invoice”, “Go to dashboard”).  
* Avoid using secondary buttons.   
* The action leads to a clear, single destination.

**When not to use a button**

* When including secondary support links (help center article, FAQs, legal links). Use hyperlinked text instead.  
* When the email is purely informational with no follow‑up action required.

**Number of buttons**

* Default: 1 primary button per email.  
* Avoid more than one button. If absolutely necessary, ensure clear hierarchy:  
* Secondary actions as text links, not additional buttons.

**Placement**

* Place the primary button once, near the top of the main body content, after the essential explanation.  
* For longer emails, you may repeat the same button once at the end, not with a new label.

# 5\. Signature

**Goal:** Clearly show who the email is from, using a simple, consistent sign‑off.

**Default signature**

* Use: Team Vinted  
* Do not add names, roles, or extra details.

**When to use the signature**

* Product updates  
* Onboarding / educational emails  
* Reminders and nudges  
* Any email written in a conversational tone (with a greeting or short narrative)

**When to omit the signature**  
Omit the signature for purely system or transactional emails, such as:

* Password resets  
* Verification codes / OTPs  
* Security alerts  
* Receipts, order confirmations, and similar system notifications


These emails can end directly after the core content (and button, if present), followed by the standard footer.

**Style and placement**  
Optional sign‑off line (use when the tone is friendly or explanatory):  
*Thanks,*  
*Team Vinted*

For more neutral or urgent messages, you can use just:  
*Team Vinted*

Place the signature before the footer and after any main content or primary CTA.

# 6\. Footer Details

**Goal:** Provide required information (company/legal/unsubscribe) in a consistent, low‑noise way.

**Content requirements**

* Company name and postal address (if required by law or company policy).  
* Why the user is receiving this email (e.g., “You’re receiving this email because you have a {Product} account.”).  
* Unsubscribe or notification preference link, where applicable.  
* Legal links (e.g., Terms of Use, Privacy Policy).  
* Support link, if relevant.  
* Links and CTAs in the footer  
* Do not add marketing‑style CTAs or additional buttons in the footer.  
* Avoid generic prompts like “Try now” or “Get started” at the very bottom.  
* If you must reference resources (help center, terms, privacy), use concise hyperlinked text, such as:  
  * “Help center”  
  * “Contact support”  
  * “Privacy Policy”  
  * “Terms and Conditions”

**Important:**

* We do not use additional CTAs or buttons in the footer that lead anywhere (no extra “Start now” or “See more” buttons).  
* The footer is for supporting information and required links only, presented via hyperlinked text, not buttons.

**Tone & style**

* Keep the footer neutral and factual.  
* Use short link labels; avoid full URLs in the copy.  
* Keep language consistent across all emails.
